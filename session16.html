Confusion matrix (revisited)

RelevantNon-relevant
Retrieved / predicted +TPFP
Not retrieved / predicted −FNTN
\[ P=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}}=P(\text{relevant}\mid\text{retrieved})\,,\qquad R=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}}=P(\text{retrieved}\mid\text{relevant}) \]

IoU, area under the PR curve, and mAP are built from these two plus overlap.

IoU recap

\(\mathrm{IoU}=|A\cap B|/|A\cup B|\). For PASCAL VOC a box is a positive if IoU ≥ 0.5; duplicate detections of the same ground truth: first TP, rest FP. Segmentation uses the same ratio on pixel sets, then averages over classes (mIoU).

The precision–recall curve

Dump every detection of one class, rank by confidence (high → low). At each rank compute running \(P\) and \(R\). Recall only stays or rises; precision zig-zags — down on an FP, up on a TP. The curve’s purpose: summarise the whole operating range, not a single threshold. A detector that is high-precision at high recall dominates one that only looks good at one point.

The lecture’s running sketch: 24 ROI proposals vs 15 ground truths — many FPs are inevitable; ranking by score is what makes AP meaningful.

Worked numerical — interpolated AP

Dataset has 5 apples. Ranked detections (confidence ↓), TP if IoU ≥ 0.5:

At rank 3 (the slide’s worked row): \(P=2/3\approx0.67\), \(R=2/5=0.4\). Interpolate: at each recall \(\hat{r}\), take the max precision to the right (any recall ≥ \(\hat{r}\)) so the orange zig-zag becomes a green monotone staircase, less sensitive to ranking jitter.

PASCAL VOC 2008 11-point AP: sample interpolated precision at \(r=0, 0.1, \ldots, 1.0\) and average. In the lecture’s completed example this came out

\[ \mathrm{AP} = \frac{5\times 1.0 + 4\times 0.57 + 2\times 0.5}{11} \approx 0.753 \]

(five of the eleven recall knots sit under precision 1.0, four under 0.57, two under 0.5). AP always lies in \([0,1]\).

✦ Exam-style method

① Rank by score. ② Mark TP/FP with the IoU rule (and the “first detection only” rule). ③ Fill cumulative P and R. ④ Interpolate. ⑤ Average at the 11 recall knots (or integrate the monotone curve). ⑥ mAP = mean over classes.

mAP

mean Average Precision = average of per-class AP. The headline number for Faster R-CNN, SSD, YOLO, etc. COCO additionally averages AP across IoU thresholds 0.50:0.05:0.95, which punishes loose boxes.

ROC space and AUC

For a binary classifier with a score, sweep the threshold and plot TPR \(=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})\) (recall, sensitivity) against FPR \(=\mathrm{FP}/(\mathrm{FP}+\mathrm{TN})\). Discrete classifiers (a hard yes/no) produce a single point.

PointMeaning
\((0,0)\)Never say positive — no false positives, no true positives.
\((1,1)\)Always say positive.
\((0,1)\)Perfect classification.
DiagonalRandom performance (TPR = FPR).

One point is better than another if it sits to the north-west (higher TPR, lower FPR, or both). Upper-right classifiers are “liberal” (weak evidence still called positive — catch all TPs, pay in FPs); lower-left are “conservative.” AUC-ROC is the area under this curve: 1 is perfect, 0.5 is random. Prefer PR / AP when positives are rare (detection); ROC is more common for balanced binary tasks.

Key takeaways

◆ Remember these
  • \(P=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP})\), \(R=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})\). IoU decides TP vs FP for boxes/masks.
  • AP = area under interpolated PR; 11-point VOC average; mAP averages AP over classes.
  • ROC: TPR vs FPR. Perfect = \((0,1)\); random = diagonal. AUC-ROC = 0.5 is chance.
  • Rare positives ⇒ trust PR/mAP, not accuracy or a single ROC point.
