Optical Flow, Lucas–Kanade & Kalman

Session 15. LK is the only optical-flow method in the exam syllabus; Kalman is conceptual plus a 1-D gain numerical — not the full matrix grind.

📄 Question

Q13(a) State the optical-flow constraint and the aperture problem. In a window, \(\sum I_x^2=10,\ \sum I_y^2=10,\ \sum I_x I_y=0,\ \sum I_x I_t=-5,\ \sum I_y I_t=-5\). Compute \((u,v)\) and classify the patch. What happens if \(\sum I_y^2=\sum I_x I_y=\sum I_y I_t=0\) with the other sums unchanged? 5M

Q13(b) Predicted position \(8\), predicted variance \(P^-=9\), measurement \(z=14\) with \(R=3\), \(H=1\). Compute the Kalman gain and the updated state. Interpret \(K\). 3M

Solution — Q13(a)

\[ I_x u + I_y v + I_t = 0 \]

One equation, two unknowns — you only recover the normal component along an edge (aperture problem). Lucas–Kanade assumes \((u,v)\) constant in a window; the left-hand matrix is Harris’s \(M\).

\[ \begin{bmatrix} \sum I_x^2 & \sum I_x I_y \\ \sum I_x I_y & \sum I_y^2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix} = -\begin{bmatrix} \sum I_x I_t \\ \sum I_y I_t \end{bmatrix} \]
Window 1
\(M=\mathrm{diag}(10,10)\), invertible, \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2=10\) → corner. \((u,v)=(-5/10,-5/10)=(-0.5,-0.5)\).
Window 2
\(M=\mathrm{diag}(10,0)\), singular → edge. Only \(u=-0.5\) is determined; \(v\) is free. Do not track edges; track corners (KLT).
Answer

Corner: flow \((-0.5,-0.5)\). Edge: not uniquely solvable. Horn–Schunck / Farnebäck are named in the slides; LK is the syllabus method.

Solution — Q13(b)

\[ K=\frac{P^-}{P^-+R}=\frac{9}{9+3}=0.75, \qquad \hat{x}=8+0.75(14-8)=8+4.5=\mathbf{12.5} \]
Answer

\(K=0.75\), updated state \(12.5\). Sensor is trusted more than the model (\(R=3<P^-=9\)), so the update sits closer to 14 than to 8. If \(R\to\infty\), \(K\to0\) (ignore the sensor). Predict then correct; DeepSORT = this + CNN embeddings + Hungarian.
