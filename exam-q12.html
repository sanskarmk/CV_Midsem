📄 Question

Q12(a) Sketch the five-step visual bag-of-words pipeline. Why a spatial pyramid? BoW vs a CNN — what is thrown away? 4M

Q12(b) You extract 500 SIFT descriptors from an image and a codebook of \(K=50\) visual words. What is the BoW representation? If you add a 2-level spatial pyramid (\(1\times1\) and \(2\times2\)), what is the feature length? 2M

Solution — Q12(a)

1 · Detect & describe
SIFT / Harris / dense grid → local descriptors (the “patches”).
2 · Codebook
k-means on a huge pool of training descriptors. The \(K\) centres are visual words (\(K\sim200\)–\(5000\)).
3 · Quantize
Each descriptor → nearest word (vector quantization).
4 · Histogram
\(K\)-bin count, then \(L_1\) or \(L_2\) normalise. Optional spatial pyramid: repeat at \(1\times1\), \(2\times2\), \(4\times4\) and concatenate.
5 · Classify
SVM / kNN / linear on the histogram.
Pyramid / vs CNN

Spatial pyramid puts a cheap layout hierarchy back: “sky words on top, road words below” without a grammar. Flat BoW ignores where the words were.

BoW vs CNN: BoW throws away precise geometry (and has a frozen codebook). A CNN learns the features and the spatial pooling. BoW still makes sense when texture words are stable and data is small (histopathology stain, Assignment 1).

Solution — Q12(b)

Flat BoW
A 50-D histogram (counts of the 500 assignments into 50 bins), then normalised. The 500 descriptors are not kept.
Pyramid \(1\times1 + 2\times2\)
\(1\) whole-image hist + \(4\) quadrant hists = \(5\times50=\mathbf{250}\) dimensions.
Answer

50-D histogram; 250-D with a 2-level pyramid. A 3-level (\(1+4+16\)) would be \(21K=1050\).

Practice questions

P12.1 Why was BoW invented for vision, then reused by NLP? 1M
Answer

Images have no obvious “words.” Quantizing SIFT patches creates a vocabulary so you can use the document-classification trick. NLP already had words; it borrowed the histogram + SVM idea the other way historically — in this course, say: same representation, different tokens.

P12.2 Semantic hierarchy vs syntactic pattern recognition — one line each, plus one use case. 2M
Answer

Ontology: poodle ⊂ dog ⊂ animal — evidence can transfer up the tree (ImageNet synsets / WordNet).

Syntactic: object as a sentence generated by a grammar of parts (bicycle = two wheels + frame); illegal parses rejected. Scene And-Or graphs.

Use case: breast-tumour patch classification (stain varies, texture words survive) or retail/apparel.

P12.3 \(K=200\), pyramid levels \(1\times1\), \(2\times2\), \(4\times4\). Feature length? 1M
Answer

\((1+4+16)\times200 = 21\times200 = \mathbf{4200}\).
