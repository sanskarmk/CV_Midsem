✦ How to answer a system-design question here

Four moves, in this order. 1 · State the constraint numerically — frame rate, latency budget, memory, stopping distance. 2 · Name the bottleneck and show the arithmetic that proves it. 3 · Choose, and reject. Every mark for “why not the other approach” is a mark most students leave on the table. 4 · State the cost of your choice and how you bound it. A design answer with no rejected alternative and no acknowledged cost reads as a memorised list.

Q1 · Real-time ADAS latency budget 10 marks

Q. A tier-1 supplier asks you to build a forward-collision-warning module. The camera streams at 30 FPS. Your current detector is a Faster R-CNN reaching good accuracy at 140 ms per image on the target SoC.
(a) Prove numerically that this fails the real-time requirement, and express the failure as a safety consequence rather than a frame count.
(b) Redesign the pipeline to meet the requirement without training a new detector.
(c) State what your redesign costs you and how you bound that cost.

(a) The arithmetic

Throughput
140 ms/image ⇒ 1000/140 ≈ 7.1 FPS. The camera delivers 30 FPS. The detector consumes one frame in the time four arrive, so the backlog grows without bound — the system is not merely slow, it is divergent.
Safety translation
Latency becomes distance. On his ladder: R-CNN's ~1760 ft stopping distance → Fast R-CNN ~176 ft → Faster R-CNN ~12 ft → YOLO under 2 ft. The design target is the average inter-car gap, 6 feet.
Verdict
At 12 ft, "by the time I see and by the time I apply the brake, even if instantaneous, I've already crashed." 12 ft > 6 ft ⇒ the module fails on safety, not on FPS.

(b) The redesign — detect-then-track

Principle
The purpose of tracking is latency reduction, not accuracy. Detect on one frame; track the object across the next 5–10 frames with a cheap classical tracker; re-detect at the 10th or 12th frame.
Cadence constraint
Keep the re-detect interval under human persistence of vision (~1/16 s) so no perceptible gap appears in the output.
Tracker choice
Lucas–Kanade sparse optical flow on Shi-Tomasi corners for short-baseline motion; Kalman for the state estimate under occlusion; mean-shift where the target is non-rigid and colour-distinct.
Effective throughput
One 140 ms detection amortised over ~10 frames of millisecond-scale tracking brings the pipeline inside the 30 FPS budget with the same detector weights.
Alternative, if retraining were allowed
Swap the two-stage detector for single-stage YOLO — 45 FPS, ~7× faster than the fastest region-proposal network, stopping distance under 2 ft. State that you rejected it here only because the question forbids a new detector.

(c) The cost, and how it is bounded

Cost
Tracker drift between detections; optical flow's three assumptions (brightness constancy, small motion, spatial coherence) all break under headlights, fast crossing traffic and camera shake.
Bounds
Shorten the re-detect interval; gate the tracked box against the next detection by IoU and re-initialise on disagreement; use a constant-acceleration Kalman (or an IMM bank) so a manoeuvring target is still modelled; run the pyramidal coarse-to-fine LK variant so large displacements fall back into the small-motion regime.
Marks live in

The 7.1-vs-30 arithmetic, the conversion of latency to stopping distance, the amortisation argument, and naming a rejected alternative with a reason.

Q2 · Why classical trackers, not DeepSORT 8 marks

Q. A shop-floor safety system must follow workers between detections. A colleague proposes DeepSORT. Argue for a conventional tracker instead, and state the one condition under which you would reverse the decision.

The objective is inverted
Tracking is not a second detector. “When it comes to tracking, we actually do not care about accuracy so much. We actually care about speed.” An approximate position is sufficient — its job is to tell the next detection roughly where to look so the detector “does not have to do heavy lifting.”
A DNN tracker attacks the wrong cost
Inserting a network into the stage whose entire purpose is latency removal defeats the stage. “It will just increase my system latency, so I've never used it.”
Deployability
Optical flow, mean-shift and Kalman are closed-form and CPU-friendly — they fit the same constrained edge box the detector already occupies. A DNN tracker wants the accelerator the detector is using.
Assumption cost, honestly stated
The classical route buys speed by assuming brightness constancy, small motion and spatial coherence (LK), or a stable colour histogram (mean-shift), or linear-Gaussian motion (Kalman). Name these as the price.
The reversal condition
When those assumptions genuinely fail and no amount of tuning recovers them — his example is particle/electron-movement tracking in cloud-chamber experiments, "where the conventional methodologies will fail." Highly specialised, not a shop floor.
Trap

Don't answer “because DeepSORT is out of syllabus.” The examinable reason is the latency argument. Also worth one line: for intra-cardiac image-guided therapy he does not track at all — he segments with U-Net. Choosing the wrong task is a bigger error than choosing the wrong tracker.

Q3 · The R-CNN family comparison 6 marks

He flagged this one directly: “this is one thing that covers the entirety of object detection, and you can assume there is a question around it… in past scenarios we have often seen that [students] are not able to answer this correctly.” (YOLO lecture 00:11:01)

Q. Compare R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN on proposal mechanism, what is shared, speed and the remaining bottleneck. Identify the single bottleneck that survives all three refinements and name the architecture that removes it.

ModelProposalsWhat is sharedSpeedBottleneck left
R-CNNSelective search, ~2000, externalNothing — one CNN pass per proposal~47 s/image inference, ~84 h training2000 forward passes; 3 separate stages; error accumulates
Fast R-CNNSelective search, still externalOne CNN pass over the whole image; RoIs projected onto the shared feature map, RoI pooling to a fixed 7×7 grid~2 s/imageProposal generation is still outside the network; RoI pooling quantisation error
Faster R-CNNRPN — learned, on the same feature map, with anchor boxesFeature map shared by RPN and both heads; RoI Align drops the rounding~140 ms/imageStill two-stage: propose, then classify

Answer

The surviving bottleneck is the region-proposal stage itself — “that's my bottleneck. We still did not resolve, and this is where YOLO steps in.” YOLO removes it by predicting class and box directly from an S×S grid in one pass.

Q4 · Edge deployment on a Raspberry Pi 7 marks

Q. A client needs on-device detection on a Raspberry Pi with a CMOS camera. No GPU. Cloud offload is contractually forbidden. Give the deployment plan and the fallback if it misses the budget.

1 · Accept the constraint
“If your use case states that it needs to stay on the edge device, it needs to stay on the edge device.” Do not design a cloud escape hatch — the constraint is the question.
2 · Pick the model tier, not the model
Nano, not large or extra-large. A YOLO nano checkpoint is ~5–6 MB and runs on CPU; large/XL are the most accurate but take the speed hit. Some nano-class models reach ~225 FPS.
3 · Convert the weights
Training saves PyTorch .pt weights, which “are not optimized for edge devices.” Export to ONNX and flash that to the device. Optimise for memory and for trainable parameter count.
4 · Recover accuracy without growing the model
Fine-tune the nano checkpoint on 100–200 annotated domain images. “You take a nano model which is barely 5 MB in size, and you fine tune it, and it gives you a performance equivalent to say a large model.”
5 · Fallback if it still misses
Write a custom implementation on a SqueezeNet backbone — ~3 MB, or a 1.5 MB variant. Then move the runtime from Python to C/C++, which is what he found necessary running YOLO 11 on a Pi.
6 · Name the alternatives you did not need
Jetson Nano, Intel OpenVINO, ARM kits — same family of targets if the client will change hardware. Say why you stayed on the Pi.
Answer shape

Tier selection → runtime conversion → transfer learning to claw back accuracy → architectural fallback → language fallback. Four of the seven marks are in steps 2 and 3.

⚠ Don't over-claim

Quantization, pruning, LoRA/QLoRA and knowledge distillation are not discussed in either of these lectures — the only optimisation levers he actually names are model tier, ONNX export, memory/parameter budgeting, a smaller backbone, and the C/C++ runtime. Mentioning quantization isn't wrong, but build the answer on what the course taught.

Q5 · Multi-camera parallelism 6 marks

Q. Six cameras stream in real time. For each deployment, choose model parallelism or data parallelism and justify: (a) a fleet of six delivery drones, one camera each; (b) one car's ADAS with six cameras around the body.

The two options
Model parallelism — train one model, replicate it across cameras, each producing an independent prediction. Data parallelism — merge the incoming streams and feed the combined data to one model.
(a) Drone fleet → model parallelism
Each drone's decision is genuinely independent — nothing camera 3 sees changes what drone 1 should do. Replicate the model per drone.
(b) ADAS → data parallelism, and model parallelism is forbidden
“You cannot have independent predictions. You need to coalesce all the information coming in from multiple cameras at one point, and then you need to predict: okay, there is an obstacle, there's an obstacle left, right.” One vehicle, one decision — the front and rear views are evidence for the same judgement, so they must meet before the prediction, not after.
Accuracy note
Asked whether the two differ in accuracy, he said they should be identical. The choice is not about accuracy — it is about where the decision is made.
The distinguishing principle

Independent decisions ⇒ replicate the model. One joint decision over several views ⇒ consolidate the data first. The same logic appears on the drone side of the Kalman discussion: multiple filters, but their predictions are brought together “in one consolidated CPU or the ECU unit” before the drone acts.

Q6 · YOLO vs VLM design decision 10 marks

Q. A manufacturing client wants to query their shop floor in natural language — “show me PPE violations in bay 3 this shift.” Recommend an architecture. Cover training cost, risk, data ownership and what you would deliver first.

The case for a VLM

When it is right
The organisation lacks training infrastructure; the task genuinely needs multiple modalities plus natural language; they are willing to let someone else own the data and just fine-tune on top; delivery speed matters more than dataset curation.

The case against, on this client

Training cost
Pre-training → use-case-specific SFT → RLHF → DPO. “Multiple steps. It takes like years to train a VLM model.” Plus heavy annotation.
Risk profile — the decisive argument
“In natural language you can get away with hallucination — okay, you make an error. But in a manufacturing plant, on a shop floor, you make one error and that can cost you life and limb.” A safety-relevant detector cannot sit on a component that invents content.
Intellectual property
“If you want to own the intellectual property rights of a certain algorithm, you'll go with YOLO.” And the commercial risk of depending on someone else's product: “tomorrow they can jack up the prices for.”
Maturity
“Right now we are in the nascent stages of VLM… YOLO is going to stay for the next few years. YOLO is pretty standard till date within manufacturing industry.”

The recommendation — the hybrid he actually lands on

Split perception from reasoning
YOLO detects, but “it does not have a thought process associated with it.” So put the language model above it, not in place of it.
The stack
Wire it through ROS topics: camera topic → YOLO → an autonomous AI agent that reasons over the detections and answers the natural-language query. Detection stays deterministic, auditable and in-house; the probabilistic layer is confined to a place where a mistake is a bad answer, not an injury.
Delivery order
Ship the YOLO detector and the violation log first — it is the safety-bearing part and the part the client owns. Layer the NL query interface on top once the detector's precision/recall are accepted.
Marks live in

Naming the four VLM training stages, the hallucination/safety argument, the IP argument, and refusing the false binary — the credit is in the hybrid, not in picking a side.

Q7 · Choosing the acceptance metric 5 marks

Q. State the acceptance metric you would write into the contract for each, and explain in one line why plain accuracy is the wrong choice in all three. (i) Tumour segmentation on MRI. (ii) Pedestrian detection for ADAS. (iii) A defect-counting station on a production line.

SystemMetricWhy
(i) Tumour segmentationIoU per pixel, and DiceThe deliverable is a region, and its area is clinically meaningful — tumour size drives the decision. Compute by pixel counting over the irregular blob, not by box geometry.
(ii) Pedestrian detectionmAP@0.5, with recall weighted above precisionA missed pedestrian is a fatality; a false positive is an unnecessary brake. Asymmetric costs mean you tune the operating point, not just report AP.
(iii) Defect countingPrecision and recall with the one-GT-one-TP rule enforcedThe output is a count, so duplicate detections on the same defect corrupt the deliverable directly — the duplicate-suppression rule is the metric, not a detail of it.
Why accuracy fails everywhere

Class imbalance. In detection, a model that predicts “no object” everywhere scores above 90% accuracy — his own example — because the overwhelming majority of candidate windows are background. Accuracy measures the easy negatives.

Q8 · IoU threshold as a design knob 6 marks

Q. You raise the acceptance threshold from IoU 0.5 to IoU 0.75. Describe the effect on the TP/FP labelling, on the precision and recall columns, and on AP. Name a use case that justifies the change and one that does not.

Labelling
Detections with IoU between 0.5 and 0.75 flip from TP to FP. The ranking by confidence is unchanged — only the verdict column moves.
Precision
Falls at every rank below the first flipped detection, because ΣFP grows while ΣTP shrinks.
Recall
Falls, and its ceiling falls: the denominator is still the ground-truth count, but fewer ground truths are ever matched.
AP
Falls — the interpolated curve is pulled down and its right-hand extent shortens. Report it as mAP@0.75; the convention exists precisely because the threshold is a reporting parameter, not a constant.
Justified
Where localisation precision is the deliverable — robotic grasping, surgical guidance, dimensional measurement, oriented bounding boxes for ADAS lane geometry.
Not justified
Presence/absence alarms — an intrusion detector, a "is anyone in the cell" interlock. Tightening the threshold there discards correct detections and buys nothing the system uses.
One more line worth writing

Papers report mAP@0.5, mAP@0.75 and a small/medium/large object breakdown together for exactly this reason — a single threshold hides where the model actually fails.

Q9 · Data strategy and annotation pipeline 8 marks

Q. A client asks “how many images do we need?” Give a defensible answer, then specify the annotation pipeline you would set up.

The sizing answer

Reframe it
It is a population-versus-sample question, not a number question: how much sample is representative of the population you will deploy into? Buildings against sky need a small sample; a specific person in a crowd, or a sequence of operations on a robotics floor, needs far more.
From scratch
Greater than 10,000, and realistically 1 million images minimum.
With transfer learning
100–200 images can be enough, because the pretrained backbone has already seen the textures and formats. This is the single biggest lever in the answer.
The honest caveat
“There are projects even if you give 10 million images they won't perform that well, but 100 defined images might just work.” Sizing is about when the model becomes generalisable, not about volume — so budget for an evaluation loop, not for a fixed image count.

The pipeline

Annotate
Roboflow for detection; Label Studio or CVAT for polygon/segmentation work. Define the class list before annotation starts, not during.
Export
COCO or PASCAL VOC — or a client-specific custom format, which is common in industry.
Split
Train / validation / test, with k-fold cross-validation if the set is small enough that a single split would be unrepresentative.
The format gotcha worth a mark
Box conventions differ between YOLO versions — some expect (xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax), others (centre-x, centre-y, w, h). Converting wrongly trains a model that silently learns offset boxes.
Preprocessing note for segmentation
Resize the image and its mask together, and keep prediction and mask dimensions identical, or the per-pixel loss is meaningless.

Q10 · Kalman / IMM under a compute budget 7 marks

Q. Design the state estimation for an autonomous quadcopter delivering payloads in variable wind. Specify the filter, the state, the noise sources, how many filters and why, and justify not using a neural network.

Filter and cycle
Kalman filter, run as predict → observe → correct. Predict the next state from the motion model; take the (noisy) measurement; correct via the Kalman gain, which weights model against measurement by their covariances.
State
Position and velocity per axis, with the state covariance matrix, state transition matrix, and Gaussian process and measurement noise.
Noise sources to name
Wind and headwind resistance, drag, manoeuvres the linear model does not anticipate, occlusion or intermittent measurement, and battery fade — which does not just add noise but shifts the plan: a 100 km range becomes 80 km.
How many filters
Not one. An IMM bank — interacting multiple models — with one filter per axis. For offline proof-of-concept work, three: X (climb from ground), Y (lateral yaw), Z. The count is bounded by available compute, which is the design constraint to state explicitly.
Keep the models linear
Constant velocity and constant acceleration models, treated as hyperparameters to test. Because “the entire system is linear, which means we can implement it using simple mathematical formulation. We do not need deep learning networks to learn the nonlinear nature behind it.”
Consolidate
Bring every filter's prediction together in one ECU before acting — the same principle as the ADAS camera fusion in Q5.
Scope note

The full Kalman mathematics is explicitly out of syllabus. Only the predict/update cycle and the design reasoning above are examinable — so spend your words on the design, not on deriving the gain.

Q11 · Diagnosing a blobby U-Net mask 5 marks

Q. Your segmentation network trains to a low loss but produces low-frequency, blobby masks with poor boundaries. Diagnose the cause and give the fix. Why does a low loss coexist with a bad mask?

Diagnosis
The encoder discards spatial detail as it downsamples; naive upsampling in the decoder cannot invent it back. Max-index unpooling zero-fills everywhere it did not record a maximum, destroying data; transpose convolution learns its own kernels but has no access to the lost high-frequency content.
Fix
Skip connections. Concatenate each encoder stage's feature map into the matching decoder stage — the U-Net pattern — so boundary detail bypasses the bottleneck. Additive residuals also smooth the loss landscape, but concatenation is what restores the edges.
Why low loss ≠ good mask
Per-pixel loss is averaged over the whole image, which is dominated by easy background. A mask that is right in the interior and wrong along a thin boundary barely moves the average. That is exactly why segmentation is accepted on IoU or Dice, not on loss or accuracy.
Check the plumbing too
Symmetric depth — N downsampling stages need N upsampling stages; image and mask resized together; per-pixel softmax retained per class rather than collapsed to a max.
